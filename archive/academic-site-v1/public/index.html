A short guide to remembering

Study less often.
Remember more.

Flashcards work because they make you retrieve what you know instead of rereading it. Pair them with a simple review schedule and twenty focused minutes a day will outperform a weekend of cramming.

Question

What is the testing effect?

Answer

Retrieving a fact strengthens memory more than rereading it.

The method

Three ideas, in order of importance

  1. 1

    Retrieve

    Cover the answer and try to produce it from memory. The effort of recalling is what builds the memory. Rereading feels productive but mostly builds familiarity.

  2. 2

    Space it out

    Review a card just as you are about to forget it, then wait longer each time it goes well. Short, spread-out sessions beat one long session on the same material.

  3. 3

    Stop early

    Finish the cards that are due and close the book. A small daily habit you can sustain is worth more than a heroic session you will not repeat.

The schedule

A simple box system

The Leitner system needs no software. Keep cards in boxes. A card you get right moves up one box and is reviewed less often. A card you get wrong goes back to the first box.

Suggested review frequency by box
BoxReviewIf correct
1Every dayMove to box 2
2Every 3 daysMove to box 3
3Every weekMove to box 4
4Every monthRetire or keep

If a card is wrong at any stage, it returns to box 1.

Minimal studying

Habits that keep it small

  • One idea per card

    If an answer needs a paragraph, split the card. Short answers are easier to judge honestly.

  • Write your own

    Turning notes into questions is itself a form of study. Borrowed decks teach less than ones you made.

  • Cap new cards

    Add about ten to twenty new cards a day. Every card you add creates future reviews.

  • Keep sessions short

    Twenty to thirty minutes, once a day. Stop when the due pile is empty.

  • Be honest

    Mark a card as missed if you hesitated or only half knew it. Easy ratings feel nice and cost you later.

  • Sleep on it

    Memory consolidates overnight. A rested morning review is worth more than a late extra hour.
